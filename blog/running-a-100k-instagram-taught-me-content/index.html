running a 100k instagram taught me content

november 8, 2026 • 3 min read

i run the instagram for explore holidays, a travel company in bahrain, and the account has more than 100k followers. that number sounds like the story, but it isn't. the story is the daily work behind it: reels, carousels and stories every day, and a monthly content calendar that has to turn into real posts, not just a plan in a document.

posting every day is a different skill from posting when you feel inspired. inspiration doesn't show up on a schedule, so you build a system instead. i learned to plan a month at a time, batch ideas when they come, and leave room for the timely stuff — a holiday, a good deal, a destination people are suddenly searching for. the calendar is the safety net. without it, there are gaps, and gaps are what kill an account.

the other thing daily posting teaches you is humility. you will make something you love and watch people scroll straight past it. then you will make something in twenty minutes, almost as an afterthought, and it will take off. after enough of that, you stop trusting your taste in the abstract and start trusting what people actually stop for. the first few seconds matter more than the rest of the video. the hook is the whole job, and the rest is just keeping the promise the hook made.

travel makes that lesson sharper, because the product is a feeling. nobody books a trip because of a list of features. they book because a place looked calm, or exciting, or like the break they needed. so the work is less about showing everything and more about showing the one moment that makes someone imagine themselves there. a quiet beach at sunset will beat a busy itinerary every time.

there's also a side of the job that never shows up on the feed. alongside the content, i do reservations, ticketing and visa work. that sounds unrelated, but it keeps the content honest. when you talk to real travellers about real trips, you learn what they actually worry about — prices, paperwork, timing — and you stop making content about what a marketer thinks travellers want. the feed gets better because the day job keeps me close to real people.

if i had to sum up what running that account taught me, it's this: content is a habit, not a talent. show up, make the thing, watch what happens, and do more of what worked. most of the advice online skips the boring middle — the calendars, the daily posting, the unglamorous editing — but the boring middle is the whole job. the followers came from that, not the other way round.
