running a 100k instagram taught me content

november 8, 2026 • 3 min read

i run the instagram for explore holidays, a travel company in bahrain, and the account has more than 100k followers. that number sounds like the story, but it isn't. the story is the daily work behind it: reels, carousels, stories, and a monthly content calendar that has to actually work.

posting every day is a different skill from posting when you feel inspired. inspiration doesn't show up on a schedule, so you build a system instead. i learned to plan a month at a time, batch ideas when they come, and leave enough room in the calendar to react to what's working.

the other thing daily posting teaches you is humility. you will make something you love and watch people scroll straight past it. then you will make something in twenty minutes, almost as an afterthought, and it will take off. the audience is honest in a way no course ever is. i stopped trying to guess what people want and started listening to what they actually stop for.

travel makes that lesson sharper, because the product is a feeling. nobody books a trip because of a list of features. they book because a place looked calm, or exciting, or like the break they needed. so the work is less about showing everything and more about showing one clear feeling at a time.

there's also a side of the job that never shows up on the feed. alongside the content, i do reservations, ticketing and visa work. that sounds unrelated, but it keeps the content honest. when you talk to the person who just booked, you learn what they actually cared about, and that goes straight back into the next post.

if i had to sum up what running that account taught me, it's this: content is a habit, not a talent. show up, make the thing, watch what happens, and do more of what worked. most of the advice online skips the showing up part, but that's the whole job.
