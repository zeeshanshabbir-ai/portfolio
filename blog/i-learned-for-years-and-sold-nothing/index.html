i learned for years and sold nothing

november 8, 2026 • 3 min read

i spent years learning ai tools. not weeks, years. courses, videos, notes, new tools every month. every time something new came out, i went and learned that too. it felt like work. it looked like work. my notes were full, my watch history was full, and my bank account was exactly where it started.

the problem was not that i was lazy. the problem was that learning had become the job. finishing a course gave me the same little hit that finishing a product should have given me, so i kept signing up for the feeling instead of the result. i could explain a lot of tools. i just had nothing to show for any of them.

i kept telling myself i was getting ready. one more tool, one more tutorial, and then i would be ready to build something real. but ready never arrived, because there was always one more thing to learn. the goalpost moved every time i got close to it, and i was the one moving it.

looking back, the loop was simple. learn something, feel good, get excited, start something, get stuck, go learn something else to fix it, and never come back. repeat that for years and you end up knowing a lot and having nothing. that was me, and i knew it, which made it worse. i wasn't fooling anyone, least of all myself.

what finally changed was embarrassingly small. i stopped trying to learn my way into a sale and started trying to build my way into one. instead of another course, i picked one small, useful thing and finished it. not perfect, not ready, just finished and public. it didn't sell right away, and that was fine. the point was that something finally existed outside my notes.

that shift changed how i learn now. i still learn, but only when a real project needs it. if i'm stuck on a workflow, i learn that one piece, use it the same day, and move on. learning is a tool again, not the whole job. the job is making something, putting it in front of people, and seeing what happens next.

if you're in the same loop, here's the honest version: you probably already know enough to start. the next course won't fix the part where nothing gets finished. pick the smallest version of the thing you want to make, finish it badly if you have to, and let that be the start. i wish i'd done it years earlier.
